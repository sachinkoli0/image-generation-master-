A practical framework

Build prompts with purpose.

Strong image prompts describe the visual decisions that matter. Work from the big picture toward the fine detail.

01 · Define the subject

Identify the main subject, age range when relevant, appearance, pose, expression, action and visual priority. Keep the description specific without adding details that do not affect the image.

02 · Direct the composition

Specify framing, camera angle, subject placement, negative space, foreground and background layers, perspective, depth and aspect ratio. Composition should tell the generator where each important element belongs.

03 · Design styling and environment

Describe clothing, fit, fabric, accessories, props, location, background materials, season or time cues, and the overall art direction. Separate subject styling from environmental styling.

04 · Control light and camera language

State the light source, direction, softness, contrast, color temperature and shadow behavior. Add lens character, focal length, depth of field and focus target only when they support the intended look.

05 · Specify post-production

Describe color grading, retouching, grain, texture, compositing, typography treatment and graphic overlays. For editorial composites, explain which elements sit in front of or behind the subject.

06 · Add constraints

Use a negative prompt to prevent common failures: distorted anatomy, malformed hands, duplicated objects, unwanted text, random logos, watermarks, inconsistent shadows or clutter. Tailor constraints to the specific image rather than pasting a generic list.

Suggested JSON structure

A useful starting structure is subject, attire, pose, scene, composition, lighting, camera, post_processing, typography, output and negative_prompt. Not every image needs every field.
